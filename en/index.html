Approach

From tension to confidence

You mainly learn to present by doing it, not by only reading about it. That is why you start practicing in small steps from the very first session. This can be one-on-one or in a safe group. A place where there is room to try things, to explore, and sometimes to get stuck for a moment. That is exactly where growth happens. And with a touch of humor, tension quickly melts away.

After an introduction conversation, I create a tailored trajectory so it matches what you or your team need to present with more calm and confidence.

In a group

Learning in a small safe group

One-day workshop — A day to experience what happens when you stand in front of a group, and to discover it is less scary than you thought. You practice several times, in a group that stays small enough for everyone to participate.

Multi-day training — More time, and therefore more depth. Between training days there is room to practice what you learned, so it truly sticks. By the end, you will have delivered a presentation you feel good about.

Private program — For a team, class, or company that wants to practice together. Because you already know each other, the focus is different: safety in an existing group asks for something different than safety between strangers. We define the content and duration together.

Personal coaching

For one specific moment

Targeted preparation for one specific moment where you are going to speak: a conference presentation, a pitch to leadership, a client conversation, or a job interview. We work at your pace on the structure of your story and your presentation materials. After that, we work on posture, voice, and confidence.

About me

Who am I, and why do I do this?

During my studies in Educational Sciences and Communication at Wageningen University & Research, I learned how to bring a message across clearly. I wanted to perform in front of an audience from an early age, and during singing and theater performances I discovered what it is like to be in the spotlight. And also what tension does to you once all eyes are on you.

During several trainings in systemic work, I learned about taking your place in your system of origin. That same question arises the moment you stand in front of a room, because an audience senses within seconds whether you take your place. If you make yourself too small, you apologize for your own story; if you make yourself too big, you claim a position that is not yours there.

And you carry more with you than you think. Beliefs you learned at home, such as just act normal or do not stand out, unconsciously shape how you stand in front of a group and how you look at your audience. We explore those beliefs together, because as long as they stay invisible, they keep pulling at you.

That requires safety, and that is always the starting point. Only when you know it is okay to fail do you dare to try something new. And once that space is there, you stand differently. You feel it immediately yourself, and your audience can see it.
