Approach

From tension to confidence

You mainly learn to present by doing it, not just by reading about it. That is why you start practising in small steps from the very first session. This can be one-to-one or in a safe group setting. A place where there is room to try things out, search, and sometimes get stuck for a moment. That is exactly where growth begins. And with a touch of humour, a lot of tension falls away.

After an introductory conversation, I create a tailored programme so it matches what you or your team need in order to present with more calm and confidence.

In a group

Learning in a small, safe group

One-day workshop — A day to experience what happens when you stand in front of a group and to discover that it is less frightening than you expected. You practise several times in a group that stays small enough for everyone to take part.

Multi-day training — More time, and therefore more depth. Between the training days, there is room to practise what you have learned so it really stays with you. By the end, you will have delivered a presentation you feel good about.

Private programme — For a team, class, or company that wants to practise together. Because you already know each other, the focus is different: safety in an existing group asks for something different than safety among strangers. We determine the content and duration together.

Personal guidance

For one specific moment

Focused preparation for one specific moment when you need to speak: a conference presentation, a pitch to leadership, a client conversation, or a job interview. We work at your pace on the structure of your story and your presentation materials. After that, we work on your posture, voice, and confidence.

About me

Who am I, and why do I do this?

During my studies in Educational Science and Communication at Wageningen University & Research, I learned how to bring a message across clearly. I wanted to be in front of an audience from a young age, and through singing and theatre performances I discovered what it feels like to stand in the spotlight. And also what tension does to you the moment everyone is looking at you.

During several trainings in systemic work, I learned about taking your place in your system of origin. That same question comes up the moment you stand in front of a room, because an audience can feel within seconds whether you are taking your place. If you make yourself too small, you apologise for your own story; if you make yourself too big, you claim a position that is not yours there.

And you bring along more than you think. Beliefs you picked up at home, such as just act normal or do not stand out, unconsciously shape how you stand in front of a group and how you look at your audience. We explore those beliefs together, because as long as they remain invisible, they keep pulling at you.

That requires safety, and that is why it is always the starting point. Only when you know you are allowed to fail do you dare to try something new. And as soon as that space is there, you show up differently. You notice it immediately yourself, and your audience sees it too.
